# Transfer learning and LLM policies, examined critically

The owner asked two speculative questions on 2026-08-08 and requested a critical, quality-gated examination: whether transfer learning has real potential here, and whether a small open language model served through vLLM could act as the battle policy, since the observations are JSON. Two verification passes over primary sources answer both; like [[rlhf-transfer]], much of the value is in saying precisely what does not apply. Every number below traces to an arXiv id, a benchmark table, or a repo file, and claims the passes could not verify are marked unverified.

## Transfer learning: relevant in one direction, a measured dead end in another

The measured record splits cleanly by distance. Within one game, transfer is the genre's best-evidenced trick: the microrts competition winner fine-tuned a small-map base policy per target map and went from under 10 percent to over 90 on them (arXiv 2402.08112), with a measured boundary, transfer to the largest maps was not effective; the Honor of Kings program measured direct training on the full hero pool as non-convergent where a curriculum of distilled teachers converges (arXiv 2011.12692); and OpenAI Five's weight-preserving surgery across engine patches is estimated by its authors to have turned a forty-month project into ten (arXiv 1912.06680).

Across games, the record is negative at our scale: fine-tuning measures negative transfer even between Atari titles (arXiv 1606.04671; Gato's held-out Boxing preferred random initialization, arXiv 2205.06175), multi-task interference cuts a diverse suite's median from 191.8 to 59.7 (arXiv 1802.01561), and the generalist successes bought their transfer with a unified observation schema and 200-million-parameter models (arXiv 2205.15241), three orders of magnitude off this project's budget.

The concrete case the owner would ask about first, vcmi-gym to fheroes2, is type-incompatible at every tensor: their observation is 12,685 floats over 20 stacks and 165 hexes with 2,312 actions, ours 634 floats over 10 stacks and 99 cells with 793, and no paper shows cross-game transfer across mismatched state schemas with measured gains in this genre. Their author's journal also records no transfer experiments even within their own project. This is a measured dead end, not an open question.

Three transfer forms are live here. First, within-game scenario curriculum and fine-tuning, which is what the training program already does and what its own measurements already endorse, the diversity remedies of [[scenario-distribution]] being exactly ProcGen-shaped (arXiv 1912.01588). Second, the imitation warm start already banked, which the literature prices generously, AlphaStar's imitation-only agent beating 84 percent of ranked players before any reinforcement (DeepMind's own report; Nature ablation deltas unverified). Third, on the [[../roadmap]] horizon, battle-to-adventure skill reuse: the adventure layer invokes battle resolution as a discrete subroutine, so the frozen battle policy composes as an option, the H-DRLN shape whose small-scale numbers are positive (94 percent where from-scratch failed, arXiv 1604.07255), with no genre-scale measurement either way and the monolithic flagships not a refutation at hobby budgets.

## A small LLM as the policy: not viable, on two independent grounds

Capability first, per-model and measured. On BALROG the 0.5-to-8-billion class the owner names scores 6 to 14 percent where GPT-4o scores 32, and GPT-4o itself flat-lines on the spatial environments (arXiv 2411.13543); SmartPlay measures open small models at roughly an eighth of GPT-4 on its harder tactics tasks (arXiv 2310.01557); and in the closest domain to ours, turn-based battling, PokeLLMon needed GPT-4 plus retrieval and self-consistency scaffolding to reach 64 percent against a heuristic bot while a LLaMA-2 policy scored 8 (arXiv 2402.01118). The one small-model success, PokeChamp, is not a policy: an 8B model priors a minimax search over about ten legal actions (arXiv 2503.04094), where this project has 793 spatial actions with 5 to 30 legal. No measured case exists of a small LLM policy matching even a heuristic bot in this genre unaided.

Throughput second, and this ground is independent of capability. A battle observation serialized as JSON is roughly three thousand tokens; measured Apple-silicon rates for a quantized 7B put one decision near nineteen seconds, and vLLM itself is CPU-only experimental on this machine, with a 7B at half precision not fitting its memory at all. One reinforcement round here is about 320,000 decisions, so the LLM policy prices it at eighteen to seventy days on the target machine, or about twenty-one hours of inference on a rented consumer GPU, against roughly twenty-five seconds measured for a comparable run with the 396,570-parameter network. That is a three-to-five-order-of-magnitude regression for a policy class whose measured play is worse.

The JSON argument itself dissolves on inspection. Structured decoding does guarantee legal, schema-valid output at near-zero serving cost (arXiv 2411.15100), but that solves formatting, which the action mask already solves for free; and on the input side the one controlled study found the encoding format of policy inputs has little influence, the benefit of language-model initialization coming from pretrained sequence weights at the hundred-million scale (arXiv 2202.01771), which argues, if anything, for the entity-token transformer already listed as this project's upgrade path in [[training-design]], not a chat model over JSON.

The steelman, kept honest: language models measurably help this class of pipeline off the hot path. Motif distills preference annotations into an intrinsic reward that beats score-maximizing reinforcement on NetHack, and its own ablation shows annotation quality rising monotonically with annotator scale, the 70B annotator decisively ahead of the 7B (arXiv 2310.00166), so even the strongest offline role argues for a frontier-class annotator rather than a local small model. Offline authoring, ability-semantics annotation, and replay judging remain plausible and cheap, with no measured result either way found for LLM-derived features improving a small game policy.

## The standing answer

Transfer learning is already load-bearing here in its one well-evidenced form, within-game curriculum plus imitation warm start, and the roadmap's battle-to-adventure composition is a plausible option-style reuse with positive small-scale precedent; cross-game weight or representation transfer is a measured dead end at this scale and schema distance. A small LLM as the battle policy fails independently on measured capability and on arithmetic, and the JSON compatibility that motivated the idea purchases nothing the mask does not already provide; the evidence-backed LLM roles in a pipeline like this are offline annotation and reward-labeling, and the measurements favor large annotators, so nothing about them is local-small-model-shaped either.
