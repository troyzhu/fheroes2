## Deployment sampling, the owner's nucleus proposal

The evening's last measurement tests trimming the sampling tail at deployment, with the owner's domain refinement: confidence judged by entropy normalized against the uniform maximum over however many actions were legal, so a five-action state and a thirty-action state read on one scale. Six schemes around one checkpoint, ten held-out matchups and the two hard rungs: full sampling 0.675, greedy 0.750, nucleus at 0.5 mass 0.713, and the entropy-adaptive nucleus 0.738 with the best hard-rung line (1.00 at 850, 0.58 at 1000). Every trimming variant reads at or above full sampling everywhere, nothing pays anywhere, and all deltas sit within one standard error at this size, so the verdict is a consistent free-win signal short of adoption power. The adaptive form is the candidate because it keeps genuine forks stochastic while converging toward greedy exactly where the policy claims confidence; a larger-n run decides the deployment default (`sampling_policies.json`).

The owner then asked the grounding question, and the honest split is: nucleus itself is grounded (Holtzman et al. introduced it for language decoding on exactly the transferable mechanism, the softmax tail is where training signal was thinnest, and low-temperature evaluation of stochastic policies is standard reinforcement-learning practice), entropy-conditioned trimming is a real family in decoding (typical sampling, Mirostat), but the specific width schedule here is this project's heuristic with no theory behind it, and its validity rests on whether the policy's probabilities are calibrated at all. So the owner's actual proposal, normalized entropy as a diagnostic, came first: `fidelity_report.py` now reports entropy over the uniform maximum on the legal set, plus a ten-bin reliability table and expected calibration error of the top action's confidence against whether it was the teacher's move.

The first reading grounds the sampler after the fact: ECE 0.02 with the policy systematically underconfident in the middle bins (predicted 0.75, actual 0.85) and exact at the extremes, so trimming the tail discards mass the calibration says was overpriced, which is why every trimming variant gained or tied (`fidelity_calibration.json`).

## What "the teacher" means in the calibration table, and the chain made explicit

The owner asked whose moves the reliability table calibrates against, and the census answer is a mixture dominated by the rule-based planner: of the 224,811 decisions in the measured corpus, 97.5 percent carry the built-in planner's action as the label (195,644 passive recordings plus 23,463 DAgger relabelings) and 2.5 percent carry root-search's choice under the owner objective (5,704 across both chairs). So "the policy is calibrated" means calibrated against the same blended label distribution it distilled from, which is the right target for the imitation question and predominantly means the planner.

The reasoning chain the calibration serves, recorded because the owner walked it: the adaptive sampler needs two premises, that high confidence is trustworthy and that tail mass is overpriced; the reliability table certifies both in teacher-currency, exact at the extremes and underconfident in the middle; entropy and top-probability are two views of one confidence axis in this near-one-hot regime, with re-binning by normalized entropy the recorded tightening; and teacher-grounded calibration is structurally silent on the residual where the policy's tail beats the teacher, the walkthrough's winning rank-four action being the recorded example, so realized play, the sampler sweep, arbitrates that trade empirically. An outcome-grounded calibration, confidence against realized return, is the measurement that would close the last gap if the thread warrants the expense.

The owner's final refinement closes the thread's epistemics: the planner is deterministic, so its conditional is a delta and calibration against it is meaningful only at the argmax. The reliability table survives as classification calibration, epistemic uncertainty about which single action the deterministic function picks, and the ECE stands in that reading. Everything distribution-shaped beyond the argmax deflates: the ideal imitator of a delta has zero entropy, all tail mass is error mass by the target's own semantics, so "trimming never hurts" is near-tautological in teacher-currency and only the play sweep carried non-trivial content; the middle-bin underconfidence is sharpening headroom, not action stochasticity, of which the target has none. Graded calibration needs a graded target, and the corpus offers three: search's per-candidate rollout values (do the policy's probability ratios track search's value gaps), outcome-grounded confidence against realized wins, which is aleatoric through combat randomness, and the prior-anchored soft targets. Against the planner, only argmax-frequency questions have answers.

## The duel replay divergence was a mislabeled side (tracked defect 43), 2026-08-07

Rebuilt at the filing commit 216dd73ca, the replay tool at side both already reproduced the duel 41 of 41 with the protocol path's terminal digest `d3047355`, so the two runEpisode wirings were byte-identical all along.

The fix stays outside the engine. `capture_replay.py` now stamps a duel as side both, `render_replay.py` derives the side from the recording's substance by treating any recording with a defender checkpoint as both-sided, so the mis-stamped vendored duel replays exactly, and both terminal records now carry the decision-stream digest, `agent_decisions_v0` over every chosen command, so a replay and a protocol episode compare decision by decision rather than only at the terminal state. Verified on this tree: the vendored duel replays 41 of 41 through both paths with equal decision digests `ab598d7a` and equal state digests `d3047355`, the three single-side replay artifacts keep their recorded digests, and the five gates pass with `agent_play/tests/test_protocol.py` now vendored, since the agent gate depends on it.

## The sharpness program: the owner's chain, tested end to end

The owner's reasoning, a deterministic teacher drives imitation toward one-hot and starves later exploration, got four remedies at three seeds each on the champion mixture, then the downstream test the chain actually cares about, warmed PPO under the owner objective from each anchor.

The imitation round sorted the levers cleanly. The confidence penalty barely moves entropy at survivable strength (normalized entropy 0.165 to 0.171 against the baseline's 0.165), because the cross-entropy pull toward a delta target dominates any beta small enough not to hurt; its light form posted the round's best ladder (0.840 against 0.785) at parity elsewhere, plausibly noise. Legal-set label smoothing raises entropy the wrong way, 0.273 made of uniform junk mass, and play collapses to 0.299 held-out through per-decision tail sampling compounding over an episode. Early stopping at epoch eight is the honest softener: normalized entropy 0.290, agreement eleven points lower, play at parity, softness shaped like the model's own uncertainty rather than injected.

The downstream round then answered the chain's real question mostly in the negative at this scale: fine-tuning deltas against each arm's own anchor read entity $-0.016/-0.026$ (train/held), light entropy arm $-0.031/-0.004$, smoothing $-0.131/-0.204$ (garbage in, garbage out), and early-stop $-0.036/+0.017$, the only positive held-out delta of the four but one standard error from zero. So the softer-clone hypothesis survives as a hint in exactly the arm whose softness is genuine, and no arm turned PPO decisively positive; the anchoring bottleneck is not primarily sharpness at these budgets. ## The normalized-entropy floor: the mechanism works, the benefit is null here

The floored probe from the champion anchor shows the controller doing exactly its job: normalized entropy dipped to 0.129 in the early iterations, the coefficient rose from 0.01 toward 0.02, entropy recovered to the 0.17 to 0.20 band, and the coefficient decayed back to baseline, all visible in the heartbeat. ## The full-apparatus correction, and the rule it writes

The owner asked whether the training conclusions were using the metrics this week built, and they were not: the long-budget verdicts rested on one held-out number and eight-episode duels. The full battery and symmetry gauge on the trio overturn the headline. The 400-iteration control did not make erosion vanish; it relocated it: commander extremes reached a perfect 1.000 and defender mirrors rose to 0.347, while the Thunk ladder's upper rungs collapsed from 1.00/0.58 to 0.79/0.12, the flagship out-of-distribution capability halved and then some, invisible to the pool-adjacent evaluations the run optimized and I quoted. Self-play at the same budget kept more of the ladder (0.75/0.33), read 0.448 held-out, and its symmetry moved to $+0.087$ excess against the anchor's $-0.258$, the first attacker-favored policy measured here.

So the corrected standing: long-budget reinforcement trades distributional coverage rather than adding it, exactly the pattern the supervised plateau taught, and no current RL arm dominates its anchor across the board. The process rule this writes into the conventions: no training verdict from single-suite reads, every reinforcement conclusion runs the full battery with quality columns, per-rung ladders, and the symmetry gauge before any claim is made, because the last two headline claims of the evening died on first contact with the apparatus built for exactly that purpose (`battery_rl_trio.json`, `symmetry_rl_trio.json`).

## The attacker lean is structure, not pathology

The owner read the symmetry trio and supplied the interpretation the gauge was missing: a slight attacker lean is what correct play should show, because the engine gives the attacker initiative. The code agrees. In `battle_arena.cpp`, `GetCurrentUnit` breaks every equal-speed comparison with an `attackingUnitsGoFirst` flag, battle start seeds the preference to the attacking side, and afterwards the preferred side alternates among same-speed units, so the attacker owns the first move of the opening exchange, which is the exchange that decides removals. That is precisely the engine's own measured $+0.071$ attacker gap.

This recasts the gauge's readings. The reference column was already the right design, the neutral point is the engine's gap rather than zero, gen1's $-0.258$ defender-leaning excess is the anomaly to explain, and the self-play policy's $+0.087$ sits just past the game's true asymmetry from above. Chasing symmetry excess toward zero would be training against the rules of the game.

## Convergence, asked for and answered

The owner asked whether any convergence analysis backed the budget verdicts, and the honest answer was no: heartbeats recorded every iteration all week and no verdict ever read their trends. `convergence_report.py` now does, splitting each series into thirds and reporting the trailing third's level shift, slope per hundred iterations, and noise, with a per-metric verdict.

The owner then corrected the first draft's ordering, and the correction is structural. The rate cannot see quality of win or loss, which the two-sided reward was designed to price, so the report reads the trained reward first and the rate last, `train_ppo.py` now emits the reward decomposed over won and lost episodes each iteration (`reward_on_wins`, `reward_on_losses`, the live wq and lq; the two archived long runs predate these fields), and the per-term gradient norms are read per module, because the loss is a weighted sum and a settled total can hide one term still moving inside it.

The gradient read pays immediately. Every policy-term and value-term module series on the self-play run is settled, and all but one on the control, the policy term's slot encoder, which sits just over the trend threshold; neither budget verdict is confounded by an optimizer still broadly in motion. The one family flagged trending everywhere, the entropy term, is trending at a tail magnitude of 0.035 against the policy term's 1.02, three percent, a reminder that a trend verdict without its magnitude column is another single-metric read. The self-play run's gradient totals also drifted down by roughly a sixth between the middle and trailing thirds while its value loss trended up, the signature of a critic settling into tracking a moving target rather than fighting it.

On the 400-iteration pair it sharpens both conclusions. The fixed-AI control had settled: training win rate converged at 0.823, policy and total losses converged, only the critic still drifting down, so the ladder collapse is a property of the solution this run converged to, and more iterations were not going to undo it. The self-play run had not settled: its win rate level was still climbing between thirds ($+0.068$ against $0.065$ noise) with critic and entropy still trending, so its 400-iteration numbers undersell wherever it was heading, and the next round's longer budgets are justified by measurement. One caveat binds the second reading: under self-play the opponent mixture is nonstationary, so training win rate is not a convergence signal there, and the loss terms carry the verdict (`convergence_long_runs.json`).
