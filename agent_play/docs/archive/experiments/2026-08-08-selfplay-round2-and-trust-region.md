The night's plan followed directly from the convergence verdicts: the 400-iteration self-play run had stopped mid-climb, so the continuation round ran the same recipe at 1000 iterations across three seeds, with a matched control trio through the identical `SelfPlayEnv` code path whose pool is the built-in AI alone, so the arms differ in nothing but who answers the other side. Every run trains from the gen1 anchor on the standard pool's first twelve matchups under the two-sided reward with warmup and floor, and every artifact carries the trust-region stamp introduced the same evening. Judgment is the full apparatus by standing rule: battery with quality columns and rungs, symmetry gauge, convergence report, and duels at forty episodes, clear of the measured $\pm 0.06$ noise band.

One apparatus fact belongs before any number below, because it bounds every comparison in this file. The frozen `policy_gen1.pt` is re-evaluated by every round's battery and duel table, and it reads held-out 0.506, 0.498, 0.529 and 0.533 across the day's four batteries, ladder 0.885 to 0.938, and duels against the built-in AI of 0.65, 0.55 and 0.75. An unchanged checkpoint therefore moves 0.03 on held-out and 0.20 on a forty-episode duel table between runs, so a comparison is only safe within one report file, and duel tables at this episode count separate arms only when they differ by more than about 0.2.

## Round two: converged, better where it trained, worse where it did not

The budget question closed first: all six runs read converged on the trained reward, the win and loss reward decompositions, the rate, and the value loss, so nothing further was left in this recipe at this distribution (`convergence_round2.json`).

On the training distribution the improvement is large and the forty-episode duels put it outside noise. Against the built-in AI the self-play trio reads 0.950, 0.975 and 0.950 where the anchor reads 0.650, and the control trio 0.850 to 0.900; against share2 and clone v4 the ordering is the same with self-play ahead of control on five of six cells (`duels_round2.json`).

The battery shows what paid for it. Held-out falls from the anchor's 0.506 to 0.385, 0.419 and 0.369 for self-play and 0.346, 0.298 and 0.342 for the control; the Thunk ladder mean falls from 0.885 into the 0.6 to 0.8 band for both arms; defender play and hordes erode everywhere; commanders hold at 0.95 to 0.99 for everyone. One suite separates the arms cleanly: real maps, where self-play sits at 0.557 to 0.568 against the anchor's 0.564, fully preserved, while the control drops to 0.516 to 0.550. Self-play pays measurably less erosion than fixed-opponent training at the same budget, replicated across seeds (`battery_round2.json`).

The symmetry gauge, read against the engine's own $+0.071$ attacker gap, moves two of three self-play seeds strongly attacker-leaning (gaps $+0.171$ and $+0.212$, the third flat) and one of three control seeds, with seed errors near 0.08, so the 400-iteration finding that self-play reshapes side behavior toward the game's true asymmetry holds directionally, with honest noise (`symmetry_round2.json`).

The reading offered as hypothesis rather than verdict: the training pool was twelve matchups, gen1's breadth came from tens of thousands of generator samples, and the pattern, training-set mastery beside held-out decay with commanders and real maps least affected, is what distribution narrowness looks like. The next round's single highest-leverage change is self-play over the full matchup generator at the same budget, and no conclusion about self-play's ceiling is licensed before that runs.

## The rounds double-check: what the caps actually are, and whether anything trips them

The owner asked what a round means in the forty that guards stalls, and the code answer is the engine's own unit: one `arena.Turns()` call per round, every unit on both sides acting if it can, counted in `agent_battle_runner.cpp`. The forty is not a cap but a sliding window, forty consecutive rounds without a death tripping the `stalemate` termination, while the hard truncation sits at `maxRounds` 100 as `round_limit`; the battery's length column is a third unit entirely, learner decisions.

`rounds_probe.py` then measured whether validation play ever approaches either bound: the anchor and all six round-two checkpoints over both matchup slices, 616 episodes with exact terminal round counts. Every episode ended in victory or defeat, no stalemates and no round-limit truncations; per-policy means sit at 8 to 10 rounds on both sets, the reinforcement-trained policies play slightly shorter battles than the anchor, and the longest battle observed anywhere was 34 rounds, against a window that needs forty deathless ones in a row. The teacher-corpus census stands beside it, 61 stalemates in 16,060 episodes. Whether the round-two training episodes themselves ever stalled is unreconstructable, heartbeats carried no termination counts, so `train_ppo.py` now emits a per-iteration termination dict and every future run answers this from its own record (`rounds_probe.json`).

## The trust-region rematch: the gates differ, the outcomes do not

The nine divergence-gated runs completed overnight, the paper's binary lower bound at threshold 0.05 and the exact total variation at 0.05 and 0.20, three seeds each at the same budget, anchor, opponent path and reward as the control trio, every artifact carrying its trust-region stamp, and the chained evaluation judged all of them by the full apparatus (`battery_rematch.json`, `symmetry_rematch.json`, `convergence_rematch.json`, `duels_rematch.json`).

The gate mechanics behaved exactly as DPPO predicts. Over the trailing hundred iterations the exact gate at threshold 0.05 blocked 7 to 13 percent of samples, its binary lower bound blocked 5 to 7 on the same threshold, the ordering the bound requires, and the exact gate at 0.20 blocked under one percent, effectively unclipped importance sampling. Three genuinely different trust regions ran, and the stamps plus the `gate_fraction` heartbeat column prove which was which.

The outcomes are statistically indistinguishable. Held-out means: ratio clip 0.329, binary 0.333, exact at 0.05 0.362, exact at 0.20 0.376, against per-arm seed spreads near 0.05, so the exact arms lead by about one standard error at best, with the near-inert 0.20 threshold best of all. Ladder means overlap completely across arms, duels against the anchor's opponents are identical at 0.875 to 0.900 versus the built-in AI, all nine runs converged, and the symmetry excesses scatter over the same attacker-leaning band as the other trained arms. Every arm shows the same specialize-onto-twelve-matchups erosion as the control, and that signature dwarfs any gate effect.

Task 48 closes measured: at this budget, in this regime, the trust-region choice is not a binding constraint, and the mild lean toward the least-gated arms reads as noise until shown otherwise. The result does not contradict the paper, whose pathology needs behavior probabilities spanning orders of magnitude; a masked categorical over 5 to 30 actions warm-started from cloning is the narrow-range case its own analysis predicts to be mild. The gates stay in the trainer behind their flags, and the honest rerun trigger is a regime where the step size is actually binding, the wide-distribution round being the first candidate.

## Round three: breadth answers half the hypothesis

The wide round changed one variable, 200 generator-sampled matchups (rejection-validated through a probe reset; the raw generator emits compositions the worker refuses) instead of twelve, and every run converged at a much lower level, training win rate settling near 0.53 against round two's 0.86, because the wide mixture is simply harder on average. Episodes are also shorter there, roughly three times the iteration rate, so the per-matchup data is thin, about a thousand decisions per matchup across a run (`convergence_round3.json`, `round3_matchups.json`).

What breadth bought, replicated across seeds: the specialization signature is gone (no arm masters its training set), the control's held-out rose from round two's 0.329 mean to 0.379, defender play roughly doubled for the control and improved for self-play, hordes improved, and self-play's real-map preservation held. What breadth did not buy: neither arm approaches the anchor, held-out 0.33 to 0.41 against gen1's 0.529 and the ladder still eroded, the two arms are no longer separable on held-out, and on the old twelve matchups, now quasi-held-out, every round-three run scores below the anchor. Symmetry excesses moved toward engine-neutral, scattered $-0.137$ to $+0.067$ (`battery_round3.json`, `symmetry_round3.json`, `duels_round3.json`).

So the narrowness hypothesis resolves as partial: distribution width removes the specialization pathology and buys coverage where round two was weakest, but a residual erosion against the supervised anchor survives breadth, which relocates the problem from the data distribution to the optimization itself. The literature name for the missing piece is already in the tree, the anchored form of [[../../rl/rl-methods]], a KL leash to the frozen anchor, the same frozen-teacher bridge the Lux season-one winner used between its stages; it has never been tested here, and it is the natural next arm: wide self-play with $\beta \, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\text{gen1}})$, judged by the same apparatus.

## The support-complete corpus exists

The coverage-forced collector ran its first corpus in the morning: six shards, three per chair, 24 sampled matchups each at 48 simulations under the owner objective, win-filtered as always, 480 episodes and 6,996 candidate-annotated decisions kept, manifests stamped `coverage_forced` beside the simulation count. 

The property the whole program waited for verifies on the records: 80 percent of decisions carry full support, every legal candidate visited at least once, with mean candidate coverage 0.976, against the roughly two-visited-candidates-per-state that nulled every prior soft-target arm (the scaled corpus reads better still, 91.8 percent full support at mean coverage 0.989, vendored as `coverage_corpus_stats.json` after the audit found these four figures quoted from standard output with no artifact behind them); the remainder are states whose candidate count exceeds the simulation budget, swept widest-prior-first by construction. The paired rerun this unblocks launched immediately, the prior visit-temperature and value-target twins at identical configuration with only the soft-root swapped to this corpus, three seeds per target, batteried behind (`coverage_shard*_manifest.json`; the corpus itself re-records from its manifests and stamps).

## Support pays, and it says which soft target was right

The rerun splits the soft-target family in two. Softness itself transformed exactly as the coverage argument predicted: the visit-derived target distribution reads 1.381 nats against the null round's 0.231, six times softer, and the value-derived one 0.581 against 0.19. Play then separates the targets. Value-derived soft labels beat their hard twin on nine of the battery's ten suites at three seeds, held-out $+0.044$, the Thunk ladder $+0.059$, defender $+0.021$, fresh $+0.024$, commanders and real maps positive, only the noisiest suite negative; individually each delta sits near one standard error, and the signal is the coherence, the same many-suites-together shape that made the owner-objective labels credible. Visit-derived targets stay null or worse even with full support, mirrors as attacker $-0.069$.

The mechanical reading: a forced sweep gives every candidate one visit, so visit counts become nearly uniform plus a concentration spike, soft but carrying little ranking information, while the per-candidate rollout means use the support directly, every legal action priced by a real playout. AlphaZero's canonical target is the right shape at AlphaZero's simulation scale and the wrong one at 48 simulations under a forced sweep; the value-softmax is what coverage actually unlocks here. Standing: the coverage prerequisite is confirmed as the unlock, the value-target arm is the live distillation lever, and the natural next steps are a champion-prior corpus at scale and the same support-complete data under the rollout-value and calibration consumers (`battery_cov_distill.json`, `cov_distill_*.json`).

## The scale test deflates the pattern and keeps the tendency

The scaled corpus, 15,007 support-complete labels across 1,192 episodes at the same prior and simulation budget, reran the identical paired distill, and the pilot's seven-of-eight coherence does not replicate. What survives at both scales: a small held-out improvement (rate $+0.032$ against the pilot's $+0.044$, reward $+0.074$ against $+0.101$), a mirrors-as-attacker improvement ($+0.025$), fidelity a third of a point up, and the visits-target null (now $-0.033$). What does not: the ladder and defender gains vanish, and two pilot signs flip outright, mirrors-as-defender from coherently negative to positive and commanders from positive to negative, which prices suite-level deltas at three-seed power at about $\pm 0.03$ and retracts the pilot's per-suite story in both directions, including its one carried negative.

One confound is recorded rather than resolved: doubling the corpus doubles the soft rows against a fixed hard set at fixed soft weight, so the scale test also shifted the loss balance, and a clean scale test would hold soft mass constant. The honest standing: value-derived soft targets on support-complete data carry a small, twice-replicated held-out tendency of about $+0.03$ to $+0.04$ with reward columns agreeing, not the broad multi-suite lift the pilot suggested; the coverage mechanism is real and cheap, the effect it currently buys is modest, and the lever moves down the promising table rather than off it (`battery_covxl_distill.json`, `covxl_distill_*.json`, `convergence_covxl_distill.json`, `covxl_*_fidelity.json`).

## The KL leash largely cures the erosion, at zero on-distribution cost

The anchored arm ran the wide recipe at $\beta$ 0.1 and 0.5, three seeds each, with round three's unleashed trio as the $\beta = 0$ control, and every run converged including the leash tension itself, $\beta = 0.1$ settling at KL 0.40 from the anchor and $\beta = 0.5$ at 0.11, with training win rate and reward tails identical across all three $\beta$ values, so the leash costs nothing on the distribution being trained.

What it buys is most of the erosion back. Held-out recovers from the unleashed 0.383 to 0.467 and 0.472 against the anchor's 0.533, roughly two thirds of the gap; the Thunk ladder recovers from 0.670 to 0.896 at $\beta = 0.5$ against the anchor's 0.906, erosion effectively gone there; commanders 0.983 against the anchor's 0.979 where the unleashed arm had dropped to 0.906; defender play edges past the anchor; and on the old twelve matchups the leashed arms duel the built-in AI at 0.500 to 0.675, far above the unleashed round's 0.275 to 0.475 though below the anchor's own 0.750 in that same table (an earlier draft of this sentence compared them against round three's anchor reading of 0.550 and claimed a lead that the artifact does not support). 

Symmetry excesses sit in the anchor's own band. The trade the full table shows: mirrors as attacker is the one suite where the unleashed arm had drifted somewhere better than the anchor, 0.248 against 0.18, and the leash pulls that back too, retention cutting both ways (`battery_round4.json`, `symmetry_round4.json`, `duels_round4.json`, `convergence_round4.json`).

The quality columns, reported beside the rates at the owner's standing insistence, refine the recovery in both directions. On the ladder the cure is complete on every column, $\beta = 0.5$ reading wq 0.655, margin 0.587 and reward 1.205 against the anchor's 0.654, 0.597 and 1.232, with the rungs themselves at 1.00/1.00/0.94/0.64 against the anchor's 0.67 top rung and the unleashed collapse to 0.14. On held-out the reward column read a recovery of about three fifths, from the unleashed $-0.140$ to $+0.07$ against the anchor's $+0.214$, even as leashed wins keep more strength than the anchor's (wq 0.477 to 0.484 against 0.460) and the margin returns positive; that reward column is corrected below and the corrected reading is more favourable, not less. 

Mirrors as defender at $\beta = 0.1$ improves reward from the anchor's $-0.477$ to $-0.067$, though mirror suites bounce at seed power; mirrors as attacker confirms the unleashed drift as genuinely better there on reward too ($-0.438$ against the anchor's $-0.589$); and commanders shows the one quality dip, loss damage 0.500 against 0.667, a conditional read over the few losses a 0.98-rate suite leaves.

Scoped honestly: one round, one budget, three seeds per arm, held-out still 0.06 below the anchor on rate and further on reward, and far below the built-in AI, so the leash is not a crossing of anything, it is the first reinforcement configuration here that trains without destroying its anchor, which is the platform the erosion verdicts said was missing. The natural next steps are adopting $\beta = 0.5$ as the standing self-play recipe, a light $\beta$ refinement around it, and longer budgets on top of the now-stable base.

## The reward column was measuring the wrong objective, and the corrected read is kinder

Completing the built-in AI's own quality columns exposed a defect in the apparatus. The AI baseline had only ever recorded win rates, so the convention of quoting the AI beside every claim could be honored on the rate alone; extending it meant computing win quality, loss quality, margin and reward for the engine's own play, and putting both sides on one scale is what surfaced the problem. `scenarios.measure` built its environment without passing `reward_margin`, so every battery `rw` column since the column existed was the hit-point margin regardless of what the checkpoint trained on, while the conventions file claimed the column followed the run's configuration. The two rewards correlate, which is why a day of verdicts read plausibly on the wrong one.

Both are fixed: `measure` takes the margin, the battery defaults to the owner's two-sided objective and stamps `reward_margin` into every report, and the AI baseline now emits the same quality columns with the two columns that do not transfer named apart, rounds and both-side decision counts rather than the battery's learner-decision length. The correction is recorded in the conventions beside the rules it belongs with.
